# EXP-000001: Cross-Asset Trend Hypothesis

## Hypothesis

**Persistent price trends contain exploitable information at medium-term horizons, and a diversified, volatility-scaled implementation may retain positive risk-adjusted expectancy after realistic costs.**

## Economic Rationale

- Behavioral biases (underreaction to information, disposition effect) create persistent trends
- Risk premia for bearing systematic trend-following risk
- Diversification across assets reduces idiosyncratic risk

## Expected Mechanism

1. Assets exhibiting strong recent momentum tend to continue trending
2. Volatility-scaled sizing normalizes risk across instruments
3. Cross-asset diversification smooths equity curve

## Universe

- ES, NQ, GC (futures)
- EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY (FX)
- SPY, QQQ (equity ETFs)
- BTCUSD, ETHUSD (crypto)

## Horizon

Medium-term (daily bars, ~3 month lookback)

## Primary Metric

- Out-of-sample Sharpe ratio > 0.5
- Maximum drawdown < 20%
- Expectancy > 0 after costs

## Falsification Criteria

**REJECT if any of:**

1. Out-of-sample expectancy <= 0
2. Cost-stressed expectancy <= 0 (at 1.5x realistic costs)
3. Performance depends on >50% of P&L from a single instrument
4. Performance disappears under modest parameter perturbation (±10%)
5. Walk-forward degradation exceeds 50% of in-sample Sharpe
6. Drawdown behavior violates risk assumptions
7. Evidence indicates look-ahead bias or data leakage

## Known Risks

- Trend following may underperform in choppy/range-bound markets
- Transaction costs may be higher than modeled
- Capacity constraints in less liquid instruments
- Regime changes may invalidate historical patterns

## Pre-existing Evidence

- Academic literature supports trend following as a systematic factor
- Cross-asset trend strategies have historically shown positive expectancy
- However, past performance does not guarantee future results
